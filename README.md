# Human Lexical-Decision Times and Layer-wise Decodability of Word Frequency in Decoder-Only Language Models
<img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TahirSher/Layer-wise-Decodability-of-Word-Frequency-in-Decoder-Only-Language-Models/main/Simple-MultiArc%20LDT%20Workflow.jpg" width="600">

Word frequency robustly shapes human lexical-decision time, yet whether internal measures derived from decoder-only language models capture item-level lexical difficulty beyond established lexical predictors and model output likelihood remains unclear. We analyzed every layer of nine base Llama, Qwen, and SmolLM models using external word--nonword probes, categorical and continuous frequency decoding, robustness controls, a controlled carrier-sentence condition, cross-model stitching, representation interventions, and five-fold out-of-fold behavioral analyses. Frequency remained strongly decodable across depth: peak binary high-versus-low accuracy ranged from 0.918 to 0.977, and peak continuous-frequency \(R^2\) from 0.671 to 0.875. In contrast, relative high-versus-low separability in word-class probe predictions peaked within layers 0--2 in every model and generally attenuated thereafter. Out-of-fold word-class probe scores showed their strongest rank correspondence with human response time within layers 0--3 (Spearman \(\rho=-0.345\) to \(-0.455\)). At the model-specific strongest controlled layers identified in the primary analysis, probe scores contributed \(\Delta R^2=0.0152\)--\(0.0223\) beyond HAL corpus frequency, word length, orthographic-neighborhood size, token count, and model-specific isolated-word log probability; output log probability alone contributed at most \(\Delta R^2=0.0012\) beyond lexical controls. Early-quarter probe probability, normalized cumulative probability, and fixed-threshold depth showed convergent associations across all models, and the principal trajectory findings survived HC3 and log-RT sensitivity analyses. These findings establish a bounded representational--behavioral correspondence: cross-fitted word-class probe scores provide an additional representation-derived index of normative lexical difficulty without implying that the base models perform lexical decision or reproduce human processing mechanisms.
